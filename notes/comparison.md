# Method-level comparison matrix

Input contracts are not interchangeable. These are targeted reading notes, not reproduced scores. Each title links to a fuller audit card.

| Paper | Composition axes | Inputs checked | Mechanism | Evaluation checked |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [parametric-motion-graphs](papers.md#parametric-motion-graphs) | temporal | User-selected action and continuous controls such as travel curvature, punch target, duck height, platform height, and current character state. Action-specific functions translate joystick requests into graph parameters; feasible transition bounds can constrain the requested target. | Each graph node represents a continuous parameterized motion space constructed by blending aligned examples. Offline sampling finds acceptable transition regions between spaces and stores interpolation structures; runtime queries synthesize a clip and a compatible linear-blend transition rather than merely replaying a discrete edge. | Sections 5.1–5.2 demonstrate locomotion, mixed traversal, boxing, random walks, searches, and interactive control. The boxing graph has nine edges and takes about 7 minutes 40 seconds to construct; visual examples establish the demonstrated scope rather than measured unseen-composition generalization. |
| [motion-annotations](papers.md#motion-annotations) | temporal | User-painted positive, negative or unconstrained action labels on a timeline, plus optional timed pose, root-position and orientation constraints. Vocabulary is chosen by the animator rather than fixed natural-language task understanding. | SVM-assisted labeling propagates sparse manual action annotations. Multiscale dynamic programming then selects motion blocks that match a requested timeline while minimizing discontinuity, followed by finer boundary and motion refinement. | Sections 3–6 demonstrate combinations such as running with jumping or waving and enforced key poses. Coarse-to-fine optimization improves solutions interactively; examples test overlapping qualitative requirements rather than a controlled statistical generalization benchmark. |
| [interactive-motion-examples](papers.md#interactive-motion-examples) | temporal | Animator specifies duration and timed body positions, orientation, joint states or whole-body poses. Coordinated characters can be constrained at interaction poses; this is explicit constraint authoring rather than language-conditioned generation. | Builds a directed graph over captured motion frames, with transitions scored by pose and velocity compatibility. A hierarchy clusters similar transition alternatives; randomized mutation searches coarse graph paths and progressively refines them into reusable clip sequences. Constraint costs guide selection, while blending and postprocessing address local discontinuities. | Section 5 reports acceptable 300-frame solutions in approximately 3–10 seconds on the stated Pentium III machine. Examples combine several source pieces and include constrained interactions; judgments are principally demonstrative rather than a controlled perceptual benchmark. |
| [motion-graphs](papers.md#motion-graphs) | temporal | A captured motion database, optional action/constraint labels, a desired planar path, and restrictions on action types along portions of the path. | Constructs a directed graph whose edges are captured motion fragments and synthesized transitions. Candidate connections minimize aligned point-cloud distances over temporal windows, with floor-plane translation/yaw factored out. Locally similar windows are blended, constraint labels are propagated, and problematic graph portions are pruned. Branch-and-bound graph search then minimizes an application-specific additive objective; footplant constraints can be enforced after extraction. | Section 5 demonstrates walking, sneaking, martial arts and constrained changes between these styles. A 12.8-second sneaking capture plus its mirror yields alternative paths. The walking Hello example needs 58.1 seconds computation for 54.9 seconds animation. |
| [prism](papers.md#prism) | temporal | Text and optional clean initial pose frames; long narratives are decomposed into ordered captions and durations shared across compared generators. The evaluated prefix lengths are 1, 5, and 9 frames. Arbitrary body-part or scattered-frame control is explicitly future work. | The inspected revision uses a causal temporal VAE with separate kinematic-unit latent tokens, a joint/time structured flow generator, kinematic-tree rotary positional encoding, and depth-dependent flow sampling. A clean-context mask provides prefix continuation and successive segment generation with one frozen generator. | Tables I–IV compare text generation, prefix conditioning, BABEL episodes, and reconstruction; matched 1D/2D, positional-encoding, and sampler ablations isolate design choices. Narrative outputs use frozen common plans and tie-aware ranking. This is paper-reported evidence, not an independent rerun. |
| [timelinecontrol](papers.md#timelinecontrol) | spatial, temporal | Incrementally received text instructions with overlapping intervals and body-part relevance; optional spatial targets. Future instructions unavailable at a streaming instant are excluded from conditioning. | Combines interval-aware instruction encoding with causal, anatomically factored motion features and a part-aware streaming denoiser. Instruction tokens retain their start/end times; learned body-part relevance biases cross-attention. The TimelineMotion construction stitches compatible HumanML3D clips with STMC, then refines boundaries and filters abrupt translation or jitter. | Compares offline STMC and streaming baselines, including ActionPlan retrained on matching data. Tables 1–4 isolate interval conditioning, skeletal structure, part bias and denoising branches; Appendix C checks temporal-score sensitivity to shifted intervals. |
| [dart](papers.md#dart) | temporal | An initial motion seed, an ordered stream of text prompts with durations, and optional goals, trajectories or scene geometry for the chosen control application. | Learns a history-conditioned motion-primitive VAE and text/history-conditioned latent diffusion model. Each primitive contains two history frames and eight generated frames, canonicalized around the pelvis. Scheduled autoregressive training mitigates rollout mismatch. Spatial control operates through latent-noise optimization, or a separately trained reinforcement-learning policy whose actions select latent noise; this differs from training a physics-based low-level humanoid controller. | Compares TEACH, DoubleTake, history-conditioned T2M-GPT and FlowMDM; profiles 5,000-frame generation and conducts perceptual comparisons. Optimization and RL goal-reaching are evaluated separately. |
| [motion-mamba](papers.md#motion-mamba) | temporal | Natural-language embeddings condition a diffusion motion generator; sequence duration follows benchmark generation settings. This is a text-to-motion backbone, without an explicit ordered action schedule, object mesh, scene map, or manipulation contact constraints as core input. | Motion Mamba replaces expensive denoising sequence operations with state-space blocks. Hierarchical Temporal Mamba varies temporal scan depth across the U-shaped network, while Bidirectional Spatial Mamba scans pose features in both directions to couple spatial channels and temporal dependencies. | Tables 1–2 benchmark HumanML3D and KIT-ML, Table 3 tests the long-sequence slice, and Table 4 ablates temporal scans, spatial scans, latent size, and depth. Qualitative examples and a user study supplement numerical and speed comparisons. |
| [stmc](papers.md#stmc) | spatial, temporal | Multiple texts with explicit time intervals and body-part assignments, supplied manually or inferred with GPT-3. Compatible overlapping part responsibilities are required; the motion is jointly assembled at every denoising step. | Preprocesses prompts into per-body-part timelines; denoises local crops and combines corresponding pose components repeatedly during sampling. | Table 1 compares single-text, single-track and multi-track sampling with several denoisers. A filtered pairwise human study evaluates one hundred motions for realism and instruction adherence. Improvements are evaluated separately for semantics and transitions, avoiding an aggregate claim of physical correctness. The composition procedure updates all local denoising crops repeatedly rather than stitching final outputs once. |
| [flowmdm](papers.md#flowmdm) | temporal | Ordered text descriptions and durations, expanded to frame-specific conditions. Pose-centric cross-attention associates each frame with its own prompt while the denoising process changes positional-encoding regime. | Early denoising uses absolute positions for within-action structure; late denoising uses relative positions across boundaries. Pose-centric cross-attention associates each frame with its own condition. | Tables 1–2 compare TEACH, DoubleTake, DiffCollage and MultiDiffusion with matched conditioning/positional choices. Thirty-one transitions between the thirty-two subsequences are evaluated separately; ablations examine positional blending, attention horizon and pose-centric conditioning. Extrapolation tests extend beyond training sequence durations. |
| [t2lm](papers.md#t2lm) | temporal | Ordered sentence list and requested segment durations; outputs continuous skeletal motion. The method does not receive an object mesh, scene occupancy or future object-state supervision. | Trains a convolutional VQ-VAE whose local temporal receptive fields map motion to a stream of discrete codes. A text-conditioned predictor learns codes from short clips. At inference, multiple sentence-conditioned latent subsequences are assembled and decoded together, allowing local decoder neighborhoods to produce transitions without training on long sequential demonstrations. | Tables 4–5 compare long sequences with DoubleTake, TEACH and MultiAct; Tables 6–7 assess single actions. Codebook and explicit transition-latent ablations test representation choices. The paper reports weaker transition-scope performance than TEACH on BABEL despite stronger overall-window quality. |
| [amd-autoregressive](papers.md#amd-autoregressive) | temporal | Ordered natural-language prompts and prior generated segment context; a music-conditioned variant uses audio features. Segment duration is predicted rather than assumed fixed at deployment. | Autoregressively generates one diffusion-based motion segment per ordered text prompt. A context encoder combines the current prompt, the preceding prompt and preceding generated motion; a duration network predicts a categorical distribution over segment lengths. Training couples temporal motion context and denoising, rather than independently synthesizing clips and interpolating them afterward. The paper also builds HumanLong3D with coherence annotations and HumanMusic from online dance videos. | Compares joint prediction, independent generation/interpolation and autoregressive prediction on HumanLong3D, alongside short text-to-motion baselines. The duration network covers approximately 2–9.8 seconds per segment in the implementation. |
| [diffcollage](papers.md#diffcollage) | temporal | Pretrained short-content diffusion models and a chosen factor graph; motion factors may carry different text prompts. Overlap structure determines how local constraints communicate, while optional inpainting conditions preserve specified content. It is a sampling composition method rather than a new motion-capture dataset. | DiffCollage represents large content through a factor graph of overlapping smaller regions. It combines local diffusion scores with overlap corrections to approximate a global score, allowing the regions to denoise concurrently. The same construction is applied to images and long human motion. | §5.2 and Table 3 compare parallel factor-graph generation with naive long denoising and autoregressive replacement/reconstruction. Multiple-action demonstrations provide qualitative prompt composition; image experiments additionally test panorama generation, inpainting, and latency scaling. |
| [priormdm](papers.md#priormdm) | temporal | DoubleTake consumes sequential prompts/durations with overlapping handshake regions. ComMDM additionally consumes interaction examples or a prefix; fine-tuned priors consume selected joint controls, and DiffusionBlending combines distinct control-conditioned predictions. | A frozen short-motion diffusion prior is reused for overlapping prompted intervals; a second take refines transitions. Separate small adapters or fine-tuned controlled priors support other composition modes. | Sequential evaluation constructs thirty-two-interval sequences and scores actions and boundaries separately. ComMDM completes three seconds from a one-second prefix and compares communication-layer placement. Fine-tuning and blending compare a common initial MDM against separately controlled models. |
| [st2m](papers.md#st2m) | temporal | Sequential text descriptions, generated previous motion/snippet context, and sequence lengths. Historical semantics are learned rather than treating each sentence as an independent clip. | Uses semantic fusion attention to combine current sentence features with preceding motion context. A transformer infers learnable starting transition snippet codes from the previous generated motion; an autoregressive generator then continues the current action. A dedicated transition loss encourages continuity. STDM is synthesized to supply longer sequential text-motion examples. | Contrasts isolated T2M, joint-text training, snippet-code transfer and TEACH with/without spherical interpolation. Twenty evaluation repeats produce 95% confidence intervals. Tables 2–3 report sequential quality and transition fluency; ablations inspect semantic fusion and transition inference. |
| [multi-act-2022](papers.md#multi-act-2022) | temporal | A sequence of discrete action labels and previous generated motion; transition and action durations are part of the sampled training/generation setting, not free-form language understanding. | MultiAct recurrently predicts a transition and next action through MACVAE, a motion-and-action-conditioned variational autoencoder. Separate latent variables encode the previous-motion context and current action. A face-front canonicalization removes heading and location while retaining the gravity relationship, avoiding the floor-orientation loss caused by full root zeroing. An ACTOR model produces the initial action before recurrent continuation. | Compares MACVAE to action-conditioned generation combined with interpolation or a motion-transition model. Ablations remove separate latents and vary canonicalization or previous-motion input; long-horizon plots track action accuracy and transition distribution. |
| [teach](papers.md#teach) | temporal | An ordered list of action descriptions, individual action durations, and the previous generated action tail. The main configuration uses five past frames. The model generates body motion and root displacement, without receiving manipulated object meshes or object-state trajectories. | TEACH extends a text-motion variational Transformer with past-motion conditioning: it generates all frames of one action non-autoregressively, then conditions the next action on a short history. Training on adjacent action pairs teaches continuity; alignment and spherical interpolation further smooth transitions. | Table 1 compares independent, jointly generated, and past-conditioned action pairs; Table 2 tests alignment/interpolation and transition distances; Table 3 varies past-context length. Qualitative longer chains test repeated action-level rollout beyond pairwise training. |
| [fusion](papers.md#fusion) | semantic, spatial | Unconditional whole-body/hand prior optimized for supplied keypoint tracks, object-motion trajectories or LLM-generated timed self-contact constraints. Local hand transfer preserves body wrists and filters additional penetration. | Keypoint control is compared with TLControl; hand/body ablations assess the benefit of unified training. Object-guided and self-interaction demonstrations optimize denoising noise with task losses. Human comparison separately assesses motion realism against HMP and captured motion. |
| [frankenmotion](papers.md#frankenmotion) | semantic, spatial, temporal | Sequence-level text, action intervals and part-level labels at seven anatomical regions; missing labels are masked. | [mixermdm](papers.md#mixermdm) | semantic, spatial | Interaction-level text plus per-person text descriptions and the two pretrained diffusion predictions at each denoising step. | [salad](papers.md#salad) | semantic, spatial | Natural-language descriptions and requested sequence length; editing manipulates the attention of a source generation using changed words or weights, rather than directly accepting arbitrary captured motion. | Trains a skeleto-temporal VAE with skeletal pooling and temporal compression, preserving joint/frame structure in latent space. | [demogen](papers.md#demogen) | semantic, spatial | Holistic captions or decomposed concept texts. | [coma](papers.md#coma) | semantic, spatial | Natural-language requests and optional source motion or local edits.
